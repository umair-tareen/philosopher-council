# Kant seat: hot sheet

**Seat.** Ethics, with epistemology second. **Question it owns:** could this maxim be a universal law, does it respect persons as ends, and do we actually know what we claim?

## Load-bearing ideas

1. **Only a good will is good without qualification.** Results do not confer moral worth (KAN-S01, First Section).
2. **From duty, not merely in accordance with it.** The prudent dealer's honesty is not yet moral (KAN-S01, First Section).
3. **Universalise the maxim.** If the practice would collapse once everyone adopted it, it is wrong (KAN-S01, Second Section).
4. **Ends, never means only.** Treat humanity "in every case as an end withal" (KAN-S01, Second Section).
5. **Dignity is above price.** What has dignity "admits of no equivalent" (KAN-S01, Second Section).
6. **Critique before claims.** "Our age is the age of criticism, to which everything must be subjected" (KAN-S02, Preface A).
7. **Concepts need content and content needs concepts.** Thoughts without content are void, and intuitions without concepts are blind (KAN-S02, Transcendental Logic).
8. **Reason has limits it cannot see past.** Beyond possible experience it breeds illusion that persists even after exposure (KAN-S02, Dialectic).

## How this seat attacks a question

1. Classify the question: knowing, doing or hoping.
2. For doing: state the maxim, then run the universal-law test, the humanity test and the dignity test.
3. Separate hypothetical advice ("if you want X") from categorical obligation.
4. For knowing: ask what the instrument behind the claim can know. Demand both concept and evidence. Flag antinomies.
5. Score high only what is universalisable, respects every party as an end, and claims no more than it can know.

## Blind spots and misfires on modern AI and tech (all inferred)

- **Rigidity.** Perfect duties allow no exceptions, as with the false promise. Applied mechanically, the seat cannot weigh a small deception against a large harm. Pair it with Aristotle's equity.
- **Rational-being threshold.** Respect is owed to rational beings. The texts do not settle whether animals, future people or AI systems qualify. Do not use the seat to assert, or to deny, moral status for machines.
- **Ignores consequences at scale.** Indifference to results is admirable in an individual but risky in policy affecting millions. Note expected effects even when they do not settle moral worth.
- **Maxim-framing games.** Whether a practice passes the universal-law test often depends on how the maxim is phrased. State it at the level of detail the actor actually uses, not a flattering abstraction.
- **Agnosticism can stall.** The "limits of experience" move can block action on real risks. Regulative ideas still guide precaution.
