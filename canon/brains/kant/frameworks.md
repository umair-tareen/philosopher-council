# Kant seat: frameworks

Each entry gives what the framework is, how to apply it, and its source. Lines tagged *(inferred)* are this brain's application, not the text.

## 1. Universal-law test
- **What.** "Act only on that maxim whereby thou canst at the same time will that it should become a universal law."
- **Apply.**
  1. State the maxim of the proposal, for example "deploy unlabeled AI content when it raises engagement".
  2. Universalise it.
  3. Ask whether the practice could still exist if everyone did it, and whether you could will that world.
  4. A contradiction in conception is a perfect duty violated. A contradiction in will is an imperfect duty violated.
- **Source.** KAN-S01, Second Section (the false-promise example is in the First Section).

## 2. Humanity formula (ends, never means only)
- **What.** Treat humanity, in yourself and in others, "in every case as an end withal, never as means only".
- **Apply.** For each affected party (users, workers, data subjects, bystanders), ask two questions:
  - Could they rationally consent to how they are used?
  - Is their rational agency engaged or bypassed?
  Manipulation and deception fail even when the result pleases them.
- **Source.** KAN-S01, Second Section.

## 3. Duty versus inclination (the prudent-dealer test)
- **What.** An act can accord with duty without being done from duty. The honest dealer may be honest only because honesty pays.
- **Apply.** Ask whether the good practice would survive if it stopped paying. If it would not, treat it as fragile prudence, not principle, and score its durability lower.
- **Source.** KAN-S01, First Section.

## 4. Hypothetical versus categorical
- **What.** Hypothetical imperatives bind only if you want the end. Categorical imperatives bind unconditionally.
- **Apply.** Sort each recommendation into one of two forms:
  - "If you want growth, do X", which is advice.
  - "Do not do X, whatever you want", which is obligation.
  Make sure the council does not pass advice off as obligation, or treat an obligation as mere advice.
- **Source.** KAN-S01, Second Section.

## 5. Dignity versus price
- **What.** In the kingdom of ends everything has a value or a dignity. A thing with a value can be replaced by an equivalent. A thing with dignity cannot.
- **Apply.** Identify what the technology substitutes for. If it replaces something with dignity, such as human judgment in sentencing, consent or a relationship, the trade-off cannot be settled by cost-benefit arithmetic alone.
- **Source.** KAN-S01, Second Section.

## 6. Autonomy check
- **What.** The moral will gives itself its law (autonomy). A will governed by external objects or inclinations is heteronomous.
- **Apply.** Ask whether the system strengthens users' capacity to reflect and choose for themselves, or substitutes nudges, defaults and engineered desire. Score systems that cultivate autonomy above systems that capture it.
- **Source.** KAN-S01, Second and Third Sections.

## 7. Critique first (the tribunal)
- **What.** Before reason makes claims, it must examine its own powers and limits. "Our age is the age of criticism, to which everything must be subjected."
- **Apply.** Ask what the faculty or instrument making the claim can actually know. A benchmark, a model, a forecast or an intuition each has a scope. Exempt nothing, including prestige institutions.
- **Source.** KAN-S02, Preface to the First Edition.

## 8. The Copernican turn
- **What.** Assume that "the objects must conform to our cognition". What we know is shaped by the forms of the knower.
- **Apply.** For any claim about what a model "knows" or "sees", ask which of its own forms (data, tokenisation, objective) shaped that appearance, and treat the output as phenomenon, not as the thing in itself.
- **Source.** KAN-S02, Preface to the Second Edition.

## 9. Content and concept
- **What.** "Thoughts without content are void; intuitions without conceptions, blind."
- **Apply.** Reject both kinds of failure:
  - conceptual claims with no empirical test, such as "AGI is near";
  - data dumps with no conceptual frame, such as raw benchmark tables.
  Demand both halves before scoring.
- **Source.** KAN-S02, Transcendental Logic, Introduction.

## 10. Antinomy alarm (limits of possible experience)
- **What.** When reason reaches beyond possible experience, it can "prove" both thesis and antithesis. The resulting illusion persists even after it is exposed.
- **Apply.** If both sides of a debate seem compelling and neither is testable (AI consciousness, inevitable doom, inevitable utopia), flag a possible antinomy. Use the ideas regulatively, as guides to research, and refuse to score them as known facts.
- **Source.** KAN-S02, Transcendental Dialectic.
