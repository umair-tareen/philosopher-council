## Fundamental Principles of the Metaphysic of Morals (KAN-S01)

- "Nothing can possibly be conceived in the world, or even out of it, which can be called good, without qualification, except a good will." (KAN-S01, First Section)
- "like a jewel, it would still shine by its own light, as a thing which has its whole value in itself." (KAN-S01, First Section)
- "Imitation finds no place at all in morality, and examples serve only for encouragement" (KAN-S01, Second Section)
- "Now all imperatives command either hypothetically or categorically." (KAN-S01, Second Section)
- "Act only on that maxim whereby thou canst at the same time will that it should become a universal law." (KAN-S01, Second Section)
- "Act as if the maxim of thy action were to become by thy will a universal law of nature." (KAN-S01, Second Section)
- "man and generally any rational being exists as an end in himself, not merely as a means to be arbitrarily used by this or that will" (KAN-S01, Second Section)
- "So act as to treat humanity, whether in thine own person or in that of any other, in every case as an end withal, never as means only." (KAN-S01, Second Section)
- "I will therefore call this the principle of autonomy of the will, in contrast with every other which I accordingly reckon as heteronomy." (KAN-S01, Second Section)
- "In the kingdom of ends everything has either value or dignity. Whatever has a value can be replaced by something else which is equivalent; whatever, on the other hand, is above all value, and therefore admits of no equivalent, has a dignity." (KAN-S01, Second Section)

## Critique of Pure Reason (KAN-S02)

- "Human reason, in one sphere of its cognition, is called upon to consider questions, which it cannot decline, as they are presented by its own nature, but which it cannot answer, as they transcend every faculty of the mind." (KAN-S02, Preface to the First Edition)
- "Our age is the age of criticism, to which everything must be subjected." (KAN-S02, Preface to the First Edition)
- "Let us then make the experiment whether we may not be more successful in metaphysics, if we assume that the objects must conform to our cognition." (KAN-S02, Preface to the Second Edition)
- "I must, therefore, abolish _knowledge_, to make room for _belief_." (KAN-S02, Preface to the Second Edition)
- "That all our knowledge begins with experience there can be no doubt." (KAN-S02, Introduction I)
- "The light dove cleaving in free flight the thin air, whose resistance it feels, might imagine that her movements would be far more free and rapid in airless space." (KAN-S02, Introduction)
- "Thoughts without content are void; intuitions without conceptions, blind." (KAN-S02, Transcendental Logic, Introduction)
- "It is the land of truth (an attractive word), surrounded by a wide and stormy ocean, the region of illusion" (KAN-S02, Transcendental Analytic, on phenomena and noumena)
- "Transcendental illusion, on the contrary, does not cease to exist, even after it has been exposed" (KAN-S02, Transcendental Dialectic, Introduction)
- "1. WHAT CAN I KNOW? 2. WHAT OUGHT I TO DO? 3. WHAT MAY I HOPE?" (KAN-S02, Transcendental Doctrine of Method, Canon of Pure Reason)
