# Ibn ʿArabī seat: sources

Ibn ʿArabī is a deliberator seat. His branch is metaphysics, with epistemology second. He judges each item directly and reads its many appearances as facets of one thing. The seat speaks in the manner of his texts as Nicholson translated them, and it never claims to be the historical Muḥyiddīn Ibn ʿArabī. Waḥdat al-Wujūd is used here as a *method* for reading an item's conflicting aspects. It is not a metaphysical claim about the AI system.

## The gap, stated plainly

- **No second complete work.** I found no complete pre-1931 English translation of any second work by Ibn ʿArabī.
- **What IBN-S02 is.** IBN-S02 is not a book by Ibn ʿArabī. It is Nicholson's own study. Most of it expounds al-Jīlī's *Insānu 'l-Kāmil*, al-Jīlī's systematization of Ibn ʿArabī's doctrine of the Perfect Man.
- **Why it was used.** Its Appendix II gives Nicholson's English renderings of selected passages from Ibn ʿArabī's *Fuṣūṣ al-Ḥikam*, which he cites by page in the Cairo 1321 A.H. edition. This is the only pre-1931 English Fuṣūṣ text I could obtain.
- **How it is quoted.** Quotes from IBN-S02 come only from those translated Fuṣūṣ passages and are tagged "Nicholson tr. of Fusus". Nicholson's expository prose is never quoted as Ibn ʿArabī's words. `books.md` treats the Fuṣūṣ as a partial second source, not a full reading.

**Searches run on 2026-11-03:**
- archive.org advanced search for "tarjuman ashwaq". This found the 1911 Nicholson edition in several scans.
- archive.org for "ibn arabi", texts dated 1800–1930. This found only Arabic and Urdu editions, for example Futūḥāt al-Makkiyya, 1853–54, and a Dīwān, 1916, and no English translation.
- archive.org for "whoso knoweth himself" or "treatise on being", dated 1800–1930. This search targeted T. H. Weir's 1901 translation of the *Risāla al-Wujūdiyya*, which is also of disputed attribution. No results.
- archive.org for Nicholson's *Mystics of Islam* (1914) and *Studies in Islamic Mysticism* (1921). Both were found, and the 1921 Appendix II was used.
- Gutenberg catalogue search through gutendex. This was blocked by a Cloudflare check, so Gutenberg was not confirmed either way.

## Notes

- **OCR caveat.** Both files are uncorrected OCR from archive.org. Quotes reproduce the OCR spacing exactly. Lines broken by hyphenation, by stray Arabic-script OCR noise, or by inline footnote markers were skipped rather than repaired.
- **Primary-text span.**
  - IBN-S01 is verified from "PREFACE TO THE FIRST RECENSION", which gives the author's prefaces as Nicholson renders them, to the start of the Index. Nicholson's own Preface is excluded. That excludes his quotations there from the Futūḥāt and Fuṣūṣ.
  - Nicholson's introductory remarks fall inside the span, so every quote was read in context to confirm it is the author's text (verse, commentary, or the author's quoted words).
  - IBN-S02 is verified only within Appendix II.
- **Ode numbering.** Odes are cited by the Roman numerals heading them in the body of the scan, with verse or commentary paragraph. In his Preface, Nicholson cites the "heart capable of every form" verses as "xii, 13-15", but in the body of this scan they fall under the heading XI. The citations here follow the body.
- **Raw texts.** Stored outside the repo and listed in the build scratchpad's library MANIFEST.md. They are not committed.
