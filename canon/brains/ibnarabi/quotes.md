# Ibn ʿArabī seat: verified quotes

Each line was copied from the downloaded OCR text of Nicholson's translations and checked against it with a whitespace-normalized substring match. The OCR's spacing quirks, such as "form :" with a space before the colon, are reproduced exactly.

**IBN-S01 lines** are Ibn ʿArabī's own words in Nicholson's 1911 translation. They come from the odes, from the author's commentary on them (which Nicholson abridges), and from the author's prefaces as Nicholson renders them. Nicholson's own preface and notes were excluded.

**IBN-S02 lines** are Nicholson's 1921 translations of passages from Ibn ʿArabī's *Fuṣūṣ al-Ḥikam*, taken only from his Appendix II. Nicholson's own expository sentences were not used.

Ode numbers follow the headings in the body of the scan. Source ids are listed in `sources.md`.

## Tarjumán al-Ashwáq (IBN-S01)

- "Whenever I mention a name in this book I always allude to her, and whenever I mourn over an abode I mean her abode." (IBN-S01, Preface to the First Recension, author's words)
- "In these poems I always signify Divine influences and spiritual revelations and sublime analogies" (IBN-S01, Preface to the First Recension, author's words)
- "Lovers lose their way in love and become entangled." (IBN-S01, ode I, v. 4)
- "The Divine Ideas, qua Ideas, exist only in the existence of the seer" (IBN-S01, ode I, commentary 3)
- "Lovers are perplexed between two opposite things" (IBN-S01, ode I, commentary 4)
- "no action is acceptable or good or fair until it hath a spirit consisting in the intention or desire of its doer." (IBN-S01, ode II, commentary 1)
- "Do not wonder at what thou seest, for thou hast beheld thyself in the mirror of a man." (IBN-S01, ode X, v. 2)
- "My heart has become capable of every form : it is a pasture for gazelles and a convent for Christian monks," (IBN-S01, ode XI, v. 13)
- "And a temple for idols and the pilgrim's Ka'ba and the tables of the Tora and the book of the Koran." (IBN-S01, ode XI, v. 14)
- "I follow the religion of Love : whatever way Love's camels take, that is my religion and my faith," (IBN-S01, ode XI, v. 15)
- "love entails the union of two opposites" (IBN-S01, ode XI, commentary 4)
- "My Beloved is three although He is One, even as the (three) Persons (of the Trinity) are made one Person in essence." (IBN-S01, ode XII, v. 4)
- "Those who worship God in the sun behold a sun, and those who worship Him in living things see a living thing" (IBN-S01, ode XII, commentary 3)
- "He who forgets Suha is not forgetful, but he who forgets the sun is forgetful." (IBN-S01, ode XLII, v. 2)
- "thy desire to obtain possession of my essence will deceive thee and make thee imagine that the form in which I appear to thee is I myself." (IBN-S01, ode XLII, commentary 8)

## Fuṣūṣ al-Ḥikam passages in Nicholson's translation (IBN-S02)

- "The inward says "No" when the outward says "I," and the outward says "No" when the inward says "I," and so in the case of every contrary, but the speaker is One, and He is substantially identical with the hearer." (IBN-S02, Appendix II, Nicholson tr. of Fusus)
- "The world of Nature is many forms in One Mirror; nay, One Form in diverse mirrors" (IBN-S02, Appendix II, Nicholson tr. of Fusus)
- "Adam (the human essence) was the very polishing of that mirror and the soul of that form" (IBN-S02, Appendix II, Nicholson tr. of Fusus)
- "He is the knowing one and the ignorant" (IBN-S02, Appendix II, Nicholson tr. of Fusus)
- "Therefore do not praise any one but yourself or blame any one but yourself." (IBN-S02, Appendix II, Nicholson tr. of Fusus)
