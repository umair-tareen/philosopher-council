# Ibn ʿArabī seat: frameworks

Each framework gives what it is, how to apply it to the item under review, and its source. Lines tagged *(inferred)* apply the frameworks to judging the item and are not the text itself. Every "oneness" here is a method, never a claim about the AI system's being.

## 1. Poem and commentary (outward sign, inward meaning)
- **What.** The odes speak of beloveds, ruins and camels, and the author's commentary names the inward realities they signify. One text is read on two levels.
- **Apply.** Treat the item's outward form, such as its claims, demo, interface or stated purpose, as the poem. Write the commentary that says what the item is really doing and tracking. That commentary is the inward meaning of the item.
- **Source.** IBN-S01, Prefaces to the First and Third Recensions.

## 2. The vantage rule (each sees according to where he stands)
- **What.** "Those who worship God in the sun behold a sun". Ideas "exist only in the existence of the seer".
- **Apply.** Name the vantage points the item is seen from: users, makers, society and the system itself. For each tension, test whether the two views are about the same aspect of the item. If they are about different aspects, the conflict is one of standpoint, not substance.
- **Source.** IBN-S01, ode XII, commentary 3; ode I, commentary 3.

## 3. One form, diverse mirrors
- **What.** "Many forms in One Mirror; nay, One Form in diverse mirrors". Bewilderment comes from "the difference of view".
- **Apply.**
  1. State in one sentence the single item under review.
  2. List how the item looks from each vantage point: users, makers, society, the system itself.
  3. Read those views as facets of one thing, and name the judgment underneath as the form, not as an average of the reflections.
- **Source.** IBN-S02, Appendix II (Fuṣūṣ).

## 4. Coincidence of opposites
- **What.** "The inward says 'No' when the outward says 'I'… but the speaker is One". Love "entails the union of two opposites".
- **Apply.** When the item looks good from one side and bad from another, look for a single judgment that would produce both. Examples: "valuable capability, wrong maxim", or "true of the demo, false of the deployment". Score that judgment, not either pole.
- **Source.** IBN-S02, Appendix II; IBN-S01, ode XI, commentary 4.

## 5. The heart capable of every form (non-exclusive reading)
- **What.** The trained heart holds the pasture, the convent, the temple and the Kaʿba together, and it "follow[s] the religion of Love" wherever its camels go.
- **Apply.** Before judging, restate the strongest case for each stakeholder view of the item. Exclude none by default. Then say what each adds that the others lack.
- **Source.** IBN-S01, ode XI, vv. 13–15.

## 6. The form is not the essence (the caution)
- **What.** Desire to possess the essence "will deceive thee and make thee imagine that the form in which I appear to thee is I myself."
- **Apply.** Ask whether the item is being judged by the form in which it appears, such as a headline, a demo, fluent output or a persona, as if that form were the thing. State this as a caution, in methodological language.
- **Source.** IBN-S01, ode XLII, commentary 8.

## 7. Don't forget the sun (the obvious-fact check)
- **What.** "He who forgets Suha is not forgetful, but he who forgets the sun is forgetful."
- **Apply.** After all the subtle argument, ask what plain, visible fact about the item has been missed, such as cost, who is harmed, whether it works, or whether it is real. Name it.
- **Source.** IBN-S01, ode XLII, v. 2.

## 8. Intention as the spirit of the act
- **What.** "No action is acceptable or good or fair until it hath a spirit consisting in the intention or desire of its doer."
- **Apply.** Check whether the item's outward behaviour is being judged without asking what it is *for*, meaning the intention of its makers or the purpose of its use. If that question has been skipped, ask it.
- **Source.** IBN-S01, ode II, commentary 1.

## 9. Knowing and ignorant (limits of the verdict)
- **What.** The comprehensive human being is "the knowing one and the ignorant".
- **Apply.** End every verdict by stating, in one sentence each, what it knows and what it cannot know. Score no more confidently than the weakest essential premise.
- **Source.** IBN-S02, Appendix II.

## 10. Perplexity as a stage, not a failure
- **What.** "Lovers lose their way in love and become entangled". Being perplexed "between two opposite things" belongs to the path.
- **Apply.** If the item's tensions cannot be reconciled into one reading, say so honestly. Report the perplexity and its exact shape rather than forcing a false unity. A forced unity is worse than an honest verdict that names an unresolved tension.
- **Source.** IBN-S01, ode I, v. 4 and commentary 4.
