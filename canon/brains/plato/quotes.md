# Plato seat: verified quotes

Every line below was copied from the downloaded Jowett text and checked by a whitespace-normalized substring match against the dialogue body only; Jowett's introductions and analyses are excluded. Source ids are in `sources.md`. Most lines are spoken by Plato's "Socrates"; the speaker is noted where it differs.

## Republic (PLA-S01)

- "I proclaim that justice is nothing else than the interest of the stronger." (PLA-S01, Book I, Thrasymachus)
- "our aim in founding the State was not the disproportionate happiness of any one class, but the greatest happiness of the whole" (PLA-S01, Book IV)
- "we affirmed that justice was doing one’s own business, and not being a busybody" (PLA-S01, Book IV)
- "Until philosophers are kings, or the kings and princes of this world have the spirit and power of philosophy, and political greatness and wisdom meet in one" (PLA-S01, Book V)
- "how will the true pilot be regarded? Will he not be called by them a prater, a star-gazer, a good-for-nothing?" (PLA-S01, Book VI)
- "the idea of good is the highest knowledge, and that all other things become useful and advantageous only by their use of this." (PLA-S01, Book VI)
- "the good may be said to be not only the author of knowledge to all things known, but of their being and essence" (PLA-S01, Book VI)
- "Now take a line which has been cut into two unequal parts, and divide each of them again in the same proportion" (PLA-S01, Book VI)
- "To them, I said, the truth would be literally nothing but the shadows of the images." (PLA-S01, Book VII)
- "the power and capacity of learning exists in the soul already" (PLA-S01, Book VII)
- "in every man there is an eye of the soul which, when by other pursuits lost and dimmed, is by these purified and re-illumined" (PLA-S01, Book VII)
- "they only dream about being, but never can they behold the waking reality so long as they leave the hypotheses which they use unexamined, and are unable to give an account of them." (PLA-S01, Book VII)
- "In heaven, I replied, there is laid up a pattern of it, methinks, which he who desires may behold, and beholding, may set his own house in order." (PLA-S01, Book IX)
- "the imitator, I said, is a long way off the truth, and can do all things because he lightly touches on a small part of them, and that part an image." (PLA-S01, Book X)
- "there is an ancient quarrel between philosophy and poetry" (PLA-S01, Book X)

## Symposium (PLA-S02)

- "human nature was originally one and we were a whole, and the desire and pursuit of the whole is called love." (PLA-S02, Aristophanes)
- "do you not see that there is a mean between wisdom and ignorance?" (PLA-S02, Diotima)
- "Then love,' she said, 'may be described generally as the love of the everlasting possession of the good?" (PLA-S02, Diotima)
- "using these as steps only, and from one going on to two, and from two to all fair forms, and from fair forms to fair practices, and from fair practices to fair notions" (PLA-S02, Diotima)
- "he will be enabled to bring forth, not images of beauty, but realities" (PLA-S02, Diotima)
