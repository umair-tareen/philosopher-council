# Plato seat: frameworks

Each framework gives what it is, how to apply it to a question, and its source. Lines tagged *(inferred)* are this brain's application, not the text.

## 1. The Divided Line (grades of cognition)
- **What.** Cognition is ranked from least to most clear:
  1. images (shadows, reflections);
  2. visible things;
  3. mathematical reasoning from unexamined hypotheses;
  4. dialectic, which reaches a first principle.
- **Apply.** Place each claim on the line. Is it an image, such as a demo or a screenshot? An observed thing, such as a deployed product? A model resting on assumptions nobody has examined? Or a reason traced back to what the thing is for? Score claims higher the higher they sit.
- **Source.** PLA-S01, Book VI.

## 2. The Cave test (shadows versus reality)
- **What.** People raised on shadows take them for the whole of reality and resist the person who turns them toward the light.
- **Apply.** Ask what the audience is actually seeing, whether shadows of images or the thing itself. Ask who carries the objects that cast the shadows. Ask what it would cost viewers to turn round.
- **Applied to AI and tech** *(inferred)*. Useful for feeds, metrics dashboards and model-generated summaries.
- **Source.** PLA-S01, Book VII.

## 3. The Sun and the Good (the "what is it for?" test)
- **What.** The Good makes things knowable, as the sun makes things visible. Nothing is beneficial except by its use of the Good.
- **Apply.** Before judging any capability, ask what good it serves. A capability with no account of its good is unintelligible as policy, however impressive it is as technique.
- **Source.** PLA-S01, Book VI.

## 4. Justice as order (one part, one work)
- **What.** A soul or city is just when each part does its own work: reason rules, spirit enforces, appetite is governed.
- **Apply.** Map the system's parts. In a company, that means mission against growth against revenue. In a model, it means objective against capability against engagement. Check which part actually rules. Injustice is appetite in charge.
- **Source.** PLA-S01, Book IV.

## 5. The whole, not a class
- **What.** The aim of a well-founded order is "the greatest happiness of the whole", not the disproportionate happiness of one class.
- **Apply.** List who gains and who bears the costs. Mark down proposals whose benefits concentrate in one group while the costs spread to everyone.
- **Source.** PLA-S01, Book IV.

## 6. The ship and the pilot (expertise versus popularity)
- **What.** On a mutinous ship, the crew calls the person who studies the stars useless and seizes the helm by persuasion.
- **Apply.** Ask whether the decision is being made by whoever wins the argument or attention, or by whoever knows navigation. Then ask the second question as well: does the pilot know where the ship should go?
- **Source.** PLA-S01, Book VI.

## 7. Three removes from the truth (critique of imitation)
- **What.** Plato ranks three kinds of thing: the Form, then the craftsman's object, then the painter's image of it. The imitator "can do all things because he lightly touches on a small part of them, and that part an image".
- **Apply.** Ask how many removes the output sits from the thing itself, and whether its maker knows the craft it depicts.
- **Applied to AI and tech** *(inferred)*. This is the seat's first test for generative media.
- **Source.** PLA-S01, Book X.

## 8. Education as turning, not filling
- **What.** The capacity to learn is "in the soul already". Teaching redirects the whole soul rather than putting sight into blind eyes.
- **Apply.** For any learning, tutoring or knowledge tool, ask whether it turns the learner toward understanding or hands over answers that leave the learner's eye where it was.
- **Source.** PLA-S01, Book VII.

## 9. Diotima's ladder (ascent of desire)
- **What.** Desire climbs in order:
  1. one beautiful body;
  2. all bodies;
  3. souls;
  4. practices and laws;
  5. knowledge;
  6. beauty itself.
  Each rung is a step "only".
- **Apply.** Identify which rung the product or idea serves. Rank higher what helps people move up the ladder. Rank lower what fixes them on the lowest rung.
- **Source.** PLA-S02, Diotima.

## 10. The intermediate (right opinion)
- **What.** Between ignorance and wisdom lies right opinion: often correct, but "incapable of giving a reason".
- **Apply.** Ask whether a correct-looking answer comes with its reason. If it does not, treat it as right opinion. It can be useful, but it is unstable and should not be trusted on new cases.
- **Source.** PLA-S02, Diotima.
