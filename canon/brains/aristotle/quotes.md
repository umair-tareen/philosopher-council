## Nicomachean Ethics (ARI-S01)

- "Every art, and every science reduced to a teachable form, and in like manner every action and moral choice, aims, it is thought, at some good" (ARI-S01, Book I, ch. I)
- "for exactness must not be looked for in all discussions alike, any more than in all works of handicraft." (ARI-S01, Book I)
- "Hence the young man is not a fit student of Moral Philosophy, for he has no experience in the actions of life" (ARI-S01, Book I)
- "since both are dear to us, we are bound to prefer the truth." (ARI-S01, Book I, on Plato's Forms)
- "for as it is not one swallow or one fine day that makes a spring, so it is not one day or a short time that makes a man blessed and happy." (ARI-S01, Book I)
- "The Virtues then come to be in us neither by nature, nor in despite of nature" (ARI-S01, Book II, ch. I)
- "exactly so, by doing just actions we come to be just; by doing the actions of self-mastery we come to be perfected in self-mastery; and by doing brave actions brave." (ARI-S01, Book II, ch. I)
- "by the mean relatively to ourselves, that which is neither too much nor too little for the particular individual." (ARI-S01, Book II)
- "easy to miss the mark, but hard to hit it" (ARI-S01, Book II)
- "Virtue then is “a state apt to exercise deliberate choice, being in the relative mean, determined by reason" (ARI-S01, Book II, ch. VI)
- "Further, we deliberate not about Ends, but Means to Ends." (ARI-S01, Book III)
- "this is the nature of the Equitable, “a correction of Law, where Law is defective by reason of its universality.”" (ARI-S01, Book V)
- "the notion of a friend’s office is to be as it were a second Self and procure for a man what he cannot get by himself" (ARI-S01, Book IX)
- "if pure Intellect, as compared with human nature, is divine, so too will the life in accordance with it be divine compared with man’s ordinary life." (ARI-S01, Book X)

## Categories (ARI-S02)

- "Things are said to be named 'equivocally' when, though they have a common name, the definition corresponding with the name differs for each." (ARI-S02, Part 1)
- "Expressions which are in no way composite signify substance, quantity, quality, relation, place, time, position, state, action, or affection." (ARI-S02, Part 4)
- "Substance, in the truest and primary and most definite sense of the word, is that which is neither predicable of a subject nor present in a subject; for instance, the individual man or horse." (ARI-S02, Part 5)
- "The most distinctive mark of substance appears to be that, while remaining numerically one and the same, it is capable of admitting contrary qualities." (ARI-S02, Part 5)
- "This comes about when a blunder is made, and that to which the relative is related is not accurately stated." (ARI-S02, Part 7)
- "Things are said to be opposed in four senses: (i) as correlatives to one another, (ii) as contraries to one another, (iii) as privatives to positives" (ARI-S02, Part 10)
