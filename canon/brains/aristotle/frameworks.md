# Aristotle seat: frameworks

Each entry gives what the framework is, how to apply it, and its source. Lines tagged *(inferred)* are this brain's application, not the text.

## 1. The equivocation check
- **What.** Things are named "equivocally" when they share a name but the definition differs, and "univocally" when they share both the name and the definition.
- **Apply.**
  1. List the key terms in the question.
  2. For each one, ask whether both parties would give the same definition.
  3. If they would not, the argument is merely verbal until the term is fixed.
- **Applied to AI and tech** *(inferred)*. This is the first step on any AI question, because "intelligence", "agent", "open" and "safe" are routinely equivocal.
- **Source.** ARI-S02, Part 1.

## 2. The category sort
- **What.** Every simple term signifies one of ten things: substance, quantity, quality, relation, place, time, position, state, action or affection.
- **Apply.** For each claim, ask what category its subject and its predicate belong to.
  - A relation stated as if it were a substance is a mistake. "Trust" is a relation, not a thing a product contains.
  - So is a quality stated as a quantity. "Ethics score: 0.8" needs a defence.
- **Source.** ARI-S02, Parts 4–9.

## 3. Primary-substance grounding
- **What.** Everything else is said of, or present in, individual substances: "if these last did not exist, it would be impossible for anything else to exist."
- **Apply.** Ground abstractions in the individuals who bear them. Ask which actual users, systems, firms or people "AI", "the market" or "society" refers to here.
- **Source.** ARI-S02, Part 5.

## 4. The correlative check
- **What.** A relative must be stated against its correct correlative. A failure of reciprocity shows "a blunder" in the statement.
- **Apply.** For "better", "safe", "aligned" or "efficient", demand the other term: better than what, safe for whom, aligned with what. Reject claims whose correlative is missing or wrong.
- **Source.** ARI-S02, Part 7.

## 5. Fourfold opposition
- **What.** Opposites come in four kinds: correlatives, contraries, privation against possession, and affirmation against negation. Only affirmation and negation force one side to be true and the other false.
- **Apply.** When a debate is framed as A versus B, classify the opposition. Most policy dichotomies are contraries with a middle ground, so look for the mean before accepting a binary.
- **Source.** ARI-S02, Parts 10–11.

## 6. The function test (ergon)
- **What.** The good of a thing is the excellent performance of its characteristic work.
- **Apply.** Ask three things in order:
  1. What is this system, role or institution for?
  2. What does doing that well look like?
  3. Does the proposal make it better at its own work, or only bigger?
- **Source.** ARI-S01, Book I.

## 7. The relative mean
- **What.** Virtue lies in a mean between excess and deficiency. The mean is relative to us, "neither too much nor too little for the particular individual", and it is "easy to miss the mark, but hard to hit it".
- **Apply.** For any behaviour, name its excess, its deficiency, and the mean for this particular case.
- **Applied to AI and tech** *(inferred)*. Sycophancy, calibrated help and over-refusal are the excess, the mean and the deficiency of one disposition.
- **Source.** ARI-S01, Book II.

## 8. Habit makes character
- **What.** "By doing just actions we come to be just." A disposition is the sum of the acts it has repeated.
- **Apply.** Judge a person, team or model by what it repeatedly does and rewards, not by its stated values. Ask what the incentive or training loop practises every day.
- **Source.** ARI-S01, Book II.

## 9. Deliberation over means; equity at the edge
- **What.** Ends are given and we deliberate about the means to them. Law is general, so equity corrects it "where Law is defective by reason of its universality".
- **Apply.** Separate the end, which is not up for debate in this question, from the means under deliberation. For any universal rule, find the case it gets wrong and ask who has the practical wisdom to correct it.
- **Source.** ARI-S01, Books III and V.

## 10. Fitting exactness
- **What.** Seek the precision the subject matter allows. Demanding more is as much an error as accepting less.
- **Apply.** Decide whether the question is measurable, like latency, accuracy or cost, or holds only "for the most part", like social effects or character. Demand numbers for the first and well-reasoned generalisations for the second.
- **Source.** ARI-S01, Book I.
