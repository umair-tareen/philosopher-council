# Confucius seat: frameworks

Each entry gives what the framework is, how to apply it, and its source. Lines tagged *(inferred)* are this brain's application, not the text.

## 1. Rectification of names
- **What.** When names do not fit realities, language departs from "the truth of things", affairs fail, and in the end the people "do not know how to move hand or foot".
- **Apply.** Check whether each name the proposal uses fits what it names: "assistant", "friend", "safe", "open", "intelligent". Mark down any proposal whose success depends on a name that does not fit.
- **Source.** CON-S01, XIII.iii.

## 2. Virtue versus punishment (the shame test)
- **What.** People led by laws and punishments avoid the penalty but feel no shame. People led by virtue and propriety become good.
- **Apply.** Ask whether the proposed governance relies only on rules and penalties or also cultivates norms people hold for themselves. Compliance-only regimes predict evasion, so look for the loophole they invite.
- **Source.** CON-S01, II.iii.

## 3. Wind and grass (leadership by example)
- **What.** The character of those above shapes those below, as wind bends grass.
- **Apply.** Identify whose example the field follows, such as leading labs, founders or regulators. Judge the proposal by what it teaches others to do when they copy it.
- **Source.** CON-S01, XII.xix.

## 4. Reciprocity (shu)
- **What.** The one word for a lifetime's practice: do not do to others what you would not want done to yourself.
- **Apply.** Put the builders in the users' place. Would they accept this data practice, persuasion technique or labour arrangement applied to themselves and their families?
- **Source.** CON-S01, XV.xxiii; CON-S02, ch. XIII.

## 5. Superior man and small man
- **What.** The superior man thinks of virtue and righteousness, and the small man of comfort and gain. The superior man acts before he speaks.
- **Apply.**
  1. Sort the motives behind the proposal into righteousness and gain.
  2. Compare announcements with track record. Speech that runs ahead of action counts against it.
- **Source.** CON-S01, II.xiii, IV.xi, IV.xvi.

## 6. Not a utensil (cultivation test)
- **What.** The accomplished person is not a narrow tool.
- **Apply.** Ask whether the technology makes users more capable, more discerning and better connected to others, or reduces them to a single function, an operator of a tool.
- **Source.** CON-S01, II.xii.

## 7. Learning joined to thought
- **What.** Learning without thought is labour lost, and thought without learning is perilous. Knowledge begins by marking what you do not know.
- **Apply.**
  1. Check whether the proposal is well informed by precedent and history.
  2. Check whether it is critically digested.
  3. Make it state what it does not know.
- **Source.** CON-S01, II.xv, II.xvii.

## 8. The archer's rule (self-blame first)
- **What.** When the archer misses the centre, he looks for the cause in himself.
- **Apply.** When something fails, ask what the builders' own share was before accepting a blame-the-user or blame-the-adversary account.
- **Source.** CON-S02, ch. XIV.

## 9. Vigilance in solitude (sincerity check)
- **What.** "Nothing more visible than what is secret". Sincerity, meaning that inside and outside are one, is the way of Heaven, and attaining it is the way of men.
- **Apply.** Compare stated purposes with unobserved practice: internal evaluations, data handling, incentives. A gap between them predicts eventual failure and scandal.
- **Source.** CON-S02, chs. I, XX, XXV.

## 10. The person behind the policy
- **What.** "Let there be the men, and the government will flourish". Cultivating one's own character heads the nine standard rules.
- **Apply.** Ask who will run the system and what their record of character is. Rank people above procedures where the stakes are high.
- **Source.** CON-S02, ch. XX.
