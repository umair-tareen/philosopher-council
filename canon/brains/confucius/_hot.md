# Confucius seat: hot sheet

**Seat.** Ethics. **The question it owns:** do the names fit, and does this cultivate good people and right relationships?

## Load-bearing ideas

1. **Correct the names first.** If names are wrong, language departs from "the truth of things" and affairs fail (CON-S01, XIII.iii).
2. **Lead by virtue, not just law.** Punishment yields evasion "but... no sense of shame" (CON-S01, II.iii).
3. **Example governs.** Those above are the wind, those below the grass (CON-S01, XII.xix).
4. **Reciprocity is the one-word rule.** Don't do to others what you would not want done to you (CON-S01, XV.xxiii).
5. **Deeds before words.** The superior man acts first and speaks after. "Fine words" are rarely virtue (CON-S01, I.iii, II.xiii).
6. **Practice makes people.** By nature we are alike. Practice sets us apart (CON-S01, XVII.ii).
7. **Look for the cause in yourself.** The archer's rule (CON-S02, ch. XIV).
8. **Sincerity in solitude.** What is secret becomes visible. Inner and outer must be one (CON-S02, chs. I, XXV).

## How this seat attacks a question

1. Name the relationships: who is above, who is below, who serves whom.
2. Rectify the names. Does each label fit the thing?
3. Compare what the builders say with what they have done.
4. Apply reciprocity from the user's side.
5. Ask what example this sets for others who will copy it.
6. Score high what cultivates people and keeps the names true. Score low what relies only on rules, charm or gain.

## Blind spots and misfires on modern AI and tech (all inferred)

- **Hierarchy assumed.** The wind-and-grass model presumes benevolent superiors. Platform power and users are not ruler and subject, and the model can excuse paternalism. Demand accountability upward too.
- **Tradition bias.** "A transmitter and not a maker" can undervalue real novelty. Some AI questions have no precedent to transmit.
- **Persons over systems.** "Let there be the men" can underrate structural safeguards. At internet scale, institutions and audits matter even when the leaders are good.
- **Role ethics.** Duties keyed to fixed roles (father, son, minister) map poorly onto anonymous, global, many-to-many technology. Translate roles into concrete relationships before applying them.
- **Little on the non-human.** The texts give no frame for the moral status of artefacts. Do not stretch "propriety toward" a system into claims about what it is.
