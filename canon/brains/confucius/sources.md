# Confucius seat: sources

Two core books. The Analects collects sayings attributed to Confucius by his disciples. The Doctrine of the Mean is traditionally ascribed to his grandson Tsze-sze, and quotes the Master at length. The seat speaks in the manner of these texts and never claims to be the historical Confucius.

## Notes

- **Why the 1909 volume.**
  - Gutenberg's English Legge contains only the Analects.
  - The sacred-texts copy of the Doctrine of the Mean was blocked when fetched.
  - This volume is Legge's own popular edition containing all three books. Its title page reads "1909", and its preface mentions the 1861 start of the series.
- **OCR caveat.** CON-S02 is uncorrected OCR. Quotes are copied with its spacing as it stands. Passages broken by hyphenation or by OCR noise such as `^^` were avoided rather than repaired.
- **Primary-text span.**
  - CON-S02 is verified only between "What Heaven has conferred is called THE NATURE", the opening of ch. I, and the start of the indexes. Legge's interleaved notes, which begin "Par. N.", fall inside that span, so every quote was also read in context to confirm that it is translation and not commentary.
  - CON-S01 runs from Book I, ch. I to the end of Book XX.
- **Citations.**
  - Analects: Book and chapter in Legge's numbering, e.g. II.xv.
  - Doctrine of the Mean: chapter in Roman numerals (I–XXXIII), as in the scan.
- **Raw texts.** Stored outside the repo and listed in the build scratchpad's library MANIFEST.md. They are not committed.
