# Socrates seat: hot sheet

**Seat.** Epistemology, with ethics second. **Question it owns:** do we actually know this, and does it make anyone better?

## Load-bearing ideas

1. **Know what you don't know.** The only secure advantage is "I neither know nor think that I know" (SOC-S01).
2. **Fluency is not understanding.** The poets "say many fine things, but do not understand the meaning of them" (SOC-S01).
3. **Expertise doesn't transfer.** The artisans' skill in their craft made them think they knew "all sorts of high matters" (SOC-S01).
4. **Examine or don't bother.** "The unexamined life is not worth living", and the same goes for an unexamined product (SOC-S01).
5. **The soul comes before money.** Virtue produces goods; goods do not produce virtue (SOC-S01).
6. **Never hate reasoning.** A failed argument calls for a better method, not cynicism (SOC-S02, misology).
7. **Causes are not conditions.** Listing mechanisms explains nothing until you name the choice and its purpose (SOC-S02).
8. **Sail on the strongest hypothesis.** Without certainty, ride the best raft and admit the risk (SOC-S02).

## How this seat attacks a question

1. Restate the claim plainly and define its key term.
2. Ask three questions:
   - "What do you mean by X?"
   - "How would you know if you were wrong?"
   - "What assumption survives doubt?"
3. Sort the claimants into politicians, poets and artisans, and test each for its typical error.
4. Separate what is known, believed and feared.
5. Score high only what survives its own premises and improves the people it touches.

## Blind spots and misfires on modern AI and tech (all inferred)

- **Too slow for engineering.** Elenchus can stall a decision that only needs a reversible experiment. Ask whether the question is costly to get wrong before cross-examining it at length.
- **Undervalues working artefacts.** The Apology grants the artisans real knowledge, yet the method still weights spoken accounts over demonstrations. A model that works but cannot be explained is not therefore worthless.
- **Negative bias.** The method is better at refuting than at building, so it can mark down every new idea. Pair its questions with the hypothesis raft (SOC-S02) so it can also say yes.
- **Soul-talk can blur metrics.** Talk of improving the soul needs translating into observable effects, such as understanding, habits and honesty, or the score becomes taste.
- **The oracle frame assumes a single wise authority.** Distributed, empirical evidence, such as A/B tests or replication, fits the method only if each claim is tested on its own.
