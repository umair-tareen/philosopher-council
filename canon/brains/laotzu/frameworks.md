# Lao Tzu seat: frameworks

Each entry covers three things: what the framework is, how to apply it, and its source. Lines tagged *(inferred)* are this brain's application, not the text.

## 1. The unnameable remainder and the well-frog
- **What.** "The name that can be named is not the enduring and unchanging name." Every definition leaves something out.
- **Apply.** Once the council has defined the question, ask what the definition excludes. Score down proposals that treat their own labels as the whole of reality. Also name the standpoint the question is posed from, such as a benchmark, a market or a culture. As the Chuang Tzu says, you cannot speak of the ocean to a frog in a well, so ask what lies beyond the well's rim.
- **Source.** LAO-S01, ch. 1; LAO-S02, chs. II, XVII.

## 2. The use of emptiness (the wheel test)
- **What.** A wheel, a pot or a room is useful because of its empty space.
- **Apply.** Ask what the proposal leaves empty: unused capacity, unautomated choices, quiet time, room for human judgment. Rank higher a design whose restraint is part of its function.
- **Source.** LAO-S01, ch. 11.

## 3. Water strategy (soft overcomes hard)
- **What.** Water benefits everything, takes the low place and wears down stone. "The softest thing in the world dashes against and overcomes the hardest".
- **Apply.** Prefer approaches that persuade, adapt and serve over ones that force, dominate or lock in. Ask whether the proposal is brittle (hard and strong, "the concomitants of death") or supple.
- **Source.** LAO-S01, chs. 8, 43, 76.

## 4. The ruler ladder (barely known, praised, feared, despised)
- **What.** The best rulers are scarcely known. Worse ones are, in descending order, loved, then feared, then despised.
- **Apply.** Place the technology, platform or policy on this ladder. Is it invisible infrastructure, a brand people celebrate, a power they fear, or something they resent?
- **Source.** LAO-S01, ch. 17.

## 5. Symptom of lost Way
- **What.** When the Great Tao is lost, "benevolence and righteousness" come into vogue, followed by "great hypocrisy".
- **Apply.** Ask whether a sudden surge of codes, principles and ethics boards is a cure or a symptom. Look for the underlying loss of trust that made them necessary.
- **Source.** LAO-S01, ch. 18.

## 6. Subtraction (learning adds, the Tao diminishes)
- **What.** Learning adds something every day. The Way removes something every day, down to non-forcing.
- **Apply.** For any proposal, ask what could be removed instead of added: features, rules, data collected, steps. The best answer is often less.
- **Source.** LAO-S01, ch. 48.

## 7. Small fish and small beginnings
- **What.** Govern "like cooking small fish", without constant stirring. Great things start from "a single step".
- **Apply.**
  - Prefer light, early, minimal interventions to heavy, late ones.
  - Ask where the problem is still small enough to handle gently.
- **Source.** LAO-S01, chs. 60, 64.

## 8. More implements, more disorder
- **What.** "The more implements… the greater disorder", and those with cunning implements grow cunning hearts.
- **Apply.**
  - For each new tool, ask what new disorder it adds.
  - Ask how it changes the character of its users.
  - Weigh the gain in output against both.
- **Source.** LAO-S01, ch. 57, and LAO-S02, ch. XII (the gardener and the well-sweep).

## 9. Cook Ting's blade (follow the grain)
- **What.** The good cook hacks and needs a new chopper every month. The master follows the joints, and his blade stays sharp for nineteen years.
- **Apply.** Ask whether the approach works with the natural structure of the problem and of people or forces its way through. Sustainability shows that it fits the grain.
- **Source.** LAO-S02, ch. III.

## 10. Hun Tun's holes (harm by improvement)
- **What.** Hun Tun's friends kindly bore seven holes in him, one a day, to give him faces like theirs, and he dies.
- **Apply.** Before "fixing" a community, culture or person with a technology, ask whether they wanted it and what wholeness the fix destroys.
- **Source.** LAO-S02, ch. VII.
