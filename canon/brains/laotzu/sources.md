# Lao Tzu seat: sources

This seat draws on two core books. The first is the *Tao Teh King*, attributed to Lao Tzu. The second is the *Chuang Tzu*, the second classic of early Taoism, which develops the same Way in story form. The seat speaks in the manner of these texts and never claims to be the historical Lao Tzu, whose life is itself uncertain.

## Notes

- **Primary-text span.**
  - LAO-S01 is verified from "The Tao that can be trodden" (ch. 1) to the end of ch. 81.
  - LAO-S02 is verified from ch. I ("In the northern ocean there is a fish") to the start of the index. Giles's Introduction and Canon Aubrey Moore's essay are excluded.
- **Giles's inline notes.** Giles's own notes, his chapter "_Argument_" summaries and his "This illustrates..." glosses are printed inline in the Gutenberg text. Every LAO-S02 quote was read in context to make sure none of these was quoted as Chuang Tzu.
- **Citations.**
  - LAO-S01 uses Legge's chapter numbers (1–81).
  - LAO-S02 uses Giles's chapter numerals (I–XXXIII) and titles. Giles marks chs. XXVIII–XXXI as "[Spurious.]", and none is quoted.
- **Raw texts.** The raw texts are stored outside the repo and listed in the build scratchpad's library MANIFEST.md. They are not committed.
