# Lao Tzu seat: verified quotes

Each line was copied from the downloaded text and checked against the translation body by whitespace-normalized substring match. Legge's parenthetical insertions are part of his translation and are kept exactly as printed. Giles prints his own notes inline in the Chuang Tzu text, so each LAO-S02 line was read in context to confirm it is the translated text and not a note. Source ids are listed in `sources.md`.

## Tao Teh King (LAO-S01)

- "The Tao that can be trodden is not the enduring and unchanging Tao. The name that can be named is not the enduring and unchanging name." (LAO-S01, ch. 1)
- "the sage manages affairs without doing anything, and conveys his instructions without the use of speech." (LAO-S01, ch. 2)
- "The highest excellence is like (that of) water. The excellence of water appears in its benefiting all things" (LAO-S01, ch. 8)
- "The thirty spokes unite in the one nave; but it is on the empty space (for the axle), that the use of the wheel depends." (LAO-S01, ch. 11)
- "In the highest antiquity, (the people) did not know that there were (their rulers). In the next age they loved them and praised them. In the next they feared them; in the next they despised them." (LAO-S01, ch. 17)
- "When the Great Tao (Way or Method) ceased to be observed, benevolence and righteousness came into vogue. (Then) appeared wisdom and shrewdness, and there ensued great hypocrisy." (LAO-S01, ch. 18)
- "He who knows other men is discerning; he who knows himself is intelligent." (LAO-S01, ch. 33)
- "The softest thing in the world dashes against and overcomes the hardest" (LAO-S01, ch. 43)
- "He who devotes himself to learning (seeks) from day to day to increase (his knowledge); he who devotes himself to the Tao (seeks) from day to day to diminish (his doing)." (LAO-S01, ch. 48)
- "the more implements to add to their profit that the people have, the greater disorder is there in the state and clan" (LAO-S01, ch. 57)
- "Governing a great state is like cooking small fish." (LAO-S01, ch. 60)
- "firmness and strength are the concomitants of death; softness and weakness, the concomitants of life." (LAO-S01, ch. 76)

## Chuang Tzu (LAO-S02)

- "Now I do not know whether I was then a man dreaming I was a butterfly, or whether I am now a butterfly dreaming I am a man." (LAO-S02, ch. II)
- "My life has a limit, but my knowledge is without limit. To drive the limited in search of the limitless, is fatal" (LAO-S02, ch. III)
- "A good cook changes his chopper once a year,--because he cuts. An ordinary cook, once a month,--because he hacks." (LAO-S02, ch. III)
- "For the perfect man employs his mind as a mirror. It grasps nothing: it refuses nothing. It receives, but does not keep." (LAO-S02, ch. VII)
- "So every day they bored one hole; but on the seventh day Hun Tun died." (LAO-S02, ch. VII)
- "those who have cunning implements are cunning in their dealings, and that those who are cunning in their dealings have cunning in their hearts" (LAO-S02, ch. XII)
- "You cannot speak of ocean to a well-frog,--the creature of a narrower sphere." (LAO-S02, ch. XVII)
- "The _raison d'être_ of language is an idea to be expressed. When the idea is expressed, the language may be ignored." (LAO-S02, ch. XXVI)
